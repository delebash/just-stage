## 1 · What CC5 shows and what ours shows (seen 2026-11-04)

CC5 HD Aaron in its live view (SubD 1): scanned skin maps - pores, redness on the nose and cheeks, stubble, eye bags,
neck creases; the eyes wet, a pink caruncle and lower lid rim, a shadow under the upper lid on the white; a dark
blue-grey stage, a strong key with soft image light, real contrast.

Ours (`naked_*.png`, the library's Average woman on a new stage): one flat skin colour over the whole body (lips and
nipples the skin's colour), no brows, faceted ear and jaw outlines (no subdivision), a bright flat eye white, a pale
washed-out picture. A dark seam line beside the nose in Cycles; a bright blotch at the navel.

## 2 · Why (the causes, not the symptoms)

1. The app has no skin maps. CC5's realism is data: scanned colour, normal, roughness and micro-normal maps. Ours is
   the chromophore model's average colour - right on average, and realism lives in the variation.
2. The face test (`tools/face_test/`) already makes textured heads near this bar (`characters_four.png`: scan base +
   painted face, real pores and colour) - it was never moved into the app. I built panels and the library around a
   placeholder skin instead. Mistake not to repeat: the character's look before features (memory "quality-first").
3. The eye stack lacks CC5's layers: the occlusion film and the tearline (the face test had both, Cycles only).
4. The stage: three area lights with a strong fill, no image light, Blender's default grey world - flat and pale.

## 5 · Every body part: the complete rec list (2026-11-04, after three research agents; attacked)

The bar (TASKS "THE BAR", the user's nine parts): realism and variety as MetaHuman's and CC5's, in the live view
(EEVEE) and the film. Sources: RESEARCH §1 (EEVEE 5.3 light nodes, skin-variety methods, SkinGen's inventory),
`2026-11-02-research-texture-source.md` §5 (HSRD-100 and the gap search), the parts agent's report (below, each fact
marked there page / API / snippet). Standard = Blender's own feature or a published method in our code; NOT STANDARD
pieces fall under the user's standing word (2026-10-17: "im ok with non standard as long as we have done the research
and this is the best way").

1. **Live-view hair shading (part 2, the worst gap).** A hair model in EEVEE 5.3's per-light nodes: Light
   Accumulation over the lights, the strand tangent from the Geometry node, the R / TT / TRT lobes (Marschner family,
   Karis 2016's real-time forms), a multiple-scattering term (Karis' or dual scattering's density fallback), and the
   world's light as its own term; the fitted colour (built) as its base. NOT STANDARD (the standard would be Blender's
   own EEVEE hair closure - a WIP pull request "not expected to land in its current state" - or cards). Attack: the
   world term double-counts if Principled stays in -> tuned against Cycles under the stage and an image light; cost
   per light per pixel -> measured; Light Accumulation on curves unverified -> the first test. One swappable node group.
2. **Head skin bases from HSRD-100 (part 1).** Its 10 people (Black men 24 and 64, Black woman 22, Hispanic man 62
   and woman 23, Asian man 25 and woman 21, white man 50, white women 60 and 41; 16K, studio, CC BY 4.0) through our
   proven scan route -> 21 scans in the mix. Attack: two wear hats or hairbands in every pose (face and neck only);
   RealDenseFace needs the card (short runs); credits listed per scan.
3. **Tone-true variety: chromophore maps (part 1).** Each scan layer inverted per texel to melanin and blood by our
   own skin model, a residual kept; characters made by shifting melanin and blood, so freckles, redness and mottling
   behave as on real skin of that tone (today a red noise reads the same on dark skin). Published method (Donner 2008,
   Aliaga 2022), our code. Attack: light left in a scan reads as melanin (the low band is already dropped); the model is
   extrapolated past NIST's darkest -> checked on the dark scans.
4. **Any described person: the face generator in the app (part 1).** The face test's painter as a command (describe
   sex, age, ethnicity, features; FLUX paints the face on the character's own head over a base matched to them; baked
   to a mapped skin) - CC5 Headshot's AI generator in kind. Attack: ~7 min of card a person; drift across views (solved
   in the face test by three views over a base); the painter's licence is a label.
5. **Age as a slider on any skin (part 1).** The fine layer matched to an age group's detail statistics per region
   (Golovinskiy 2006, "adjusting the apparent age of faces"), from our old scans (yaro13, Ammamma, HSR0023, HSR0112).
6. **SkinGen-style layers (part 1, 9).** A layer stack in the skin material (blend modes, masks, a strength each):
   freckles as a point process (Schneider 2018's model), moles, age spots, acne, capillaries, veins, scars, tattoos,
   stubble shadow; lip lines deepening with age, ear translucency, areola glands. Standard: Blender's node layers;
   the structure follows SkinGen's published behaviour.
7. **Body skin (part 1).** HSRD-100's bodies (A-pose-like standing, mostly bare) onto our body: correspondence by body
   keypoints on renders (Sapiens2, already cached) then the same wrap and bake; regional offsets (elbows and knees
   darker, forearm redness with age). Attack: pose differences (arms, hands) - if the wrap fails, regional detail
   tiles cut from the 16K atlases instead.
8. **Brows and lashes (part 4).** A strand brow generator (one hair per follicle on a shape mask, direction nearly
   vertical at the head turning horizontal toward the tail, thinner at the tail), shapes from MakeHuman's 14 CC0 brows
   (`eyebrows01`) fitted to each face's brow; lashes get MetaHuman's 7 style parameters. Attack: those masks read heavy
   and high on our faces before -> fitted to landmarks.
9. **Facial hair (part 3).** Beards and moustaches as density masks (MakeHuman `bodyparts05`, CC0) with a growth
   field and per-style length; stubble as skin layer under ~1 mm plus 1-3 mm strands for close-ups. Attack: densities
   are snippet-level -> checked against scan photos.
10. **Body hair (part 5).** MakeHuman's `fur.blend` (CC0, 15 regions, inside the pack we already fetch) with masks by
    sex and age. Attack: likely the pack's tufting and one colour -> per-strand colour (also for scalp hair).
11. **Scalp styles (part 2).** Our generator on Blender's hair node groups: coily hair by Curly-Cue's method (its MIT
    guides-to-full code), braids by Blender's Braid node group, locs, buzz cuts, balding masks (Norwood / Ludwig
    stages); grey as a per-strand whiteness share, dye and ombre (MetaHuman's controls); per-strand colour variance.
    Attack: 200K+ coiled strands are too heavy for the live view -> cards for coily styles there.
12. **Eyes (part 6).** Iris features on MPFB2's eye - crypts, furrows, pigment spots, nodules, collarette,
    heterochromia (each eye its own colour: the command takes an eye), arcus with age - drawn by population (Edwards
    2016's table); sclera veins, redness, yellowing with age. Attack: sub-pixel in the live view at distance (worth it
    in close-ups, CC5's and MetaHuman's bar).
13. **Mouth (part 7).** Teeth shape variety from GNM's 80 teeth directions (on disk, unused), per-tooth gaps, wear and
    gum recession; tooth shade in VITA order darkening with age; gum pigmentation from the skin's melanin; tongue
    papillae and coating. Attack: whether GNM labels teeth individually -> checked first.
14. **Nails (part 8).** A layered nail shader on tile 1005 (blood-coloured bed, clear plate, lunula, white free edge,
    cuticle, ridges and yellowing with age); MakeHuman `bodyparts04` (CC0) nail meshes for long nails.

Downloads (all open): MakeHuman `eyebrows01`, `bodyparts04`, `bodyparts05`, `eyelashes01` (CC0), `fur.blend` (CC0, pin

### 6a · The gate, first run (2026-11-04) - our maps in three engines

Tooling: `tools/gate/` (Blender prepare / render, Unreal import / scene / render-queue, the comparison); outputs in
`.dev/gate/` (renders, `sheets/sheet_face.png`, `sheets/crop_*.png`, `sheets/report.json`). Content identical in all
three (the shipped Daniel skin on the library's Average man, hair hidden, body mottle off; Unreal gets the same maps
baked - colour x tone gain, the fine height folded into the normal map, our pore tile in MetaHuman's micro slot); the
one factor is the shading. Lights matched by linearity: each light rendered alone, each engine's scale read off an 18%
grey sphere's lit side, Unreal's stage the sum of its scaled singles; all EXRs through the same AgX look.

- **EEVEE vs Cycles, the sphere (each light alone):** key 1.006, rim 0.91 (face) / 0.84 (stage), image light 1.20 /
  1.23 (Cycles over EEVEE: EEVEE's world light ~17-19% weaker on a plain diffuse sphere - the "world light is indirect
  in EEVEE" gap, measured outside the hair). EEVEE's stage equals the sum of its singles (0.98-0.99), Cycles' (1.000).
- **The face crops (linear mean / fine-detail energy):** cheek 0.683 / 0.047 EEVEE, 0.661 / 0.046 Cycles, 0.721 /
  0.052 Unreal; nose 1.14 / 0.034, 1.13 / 0.034, 1.19 / 0.035; forehead 1.05 / 0.050, 1.02 / 0.048, 1.10 / 0.056;
  under the eye 0.531 / 0.081, 0.530 / 0.067, 0.544 / 0.084; jaw shadow 0.070 / 0.094, 0.082 / 0.073, 0.056 / 0.197.
- **Read:** lit skin agrees across the three; the shadows part - Cycles soft and warm, EEVEE darker and harsher (jaw
  -15%, +28% contrast; under the eye +21% contrast), Unreal on MetaHuman's material darker still and with pores shining
  in shadow. MetaHuman's real-time shading on our maps is not more real than our own film render: the engine is not
  what separates us from MetaHuman - the maps are, and EEVEE's one measured shading gap is its shadow softening
  (subsurface and world fill - rec 16).
- **Not yet:** the MetaHuman preset under the same set-up (the benchmark that compares MetaHuman's maps with ours);
  Unreal's eyes are placeholders; the shadow comparison carries a lighting difference (Lumen's fill vs path tracing);
